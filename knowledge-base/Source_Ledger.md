## The four lineages, and what each is actually for

They are not competing accounts. They answer different questions, and the base needs all four.

**Oscar Ichazo** *(1960s–70s, Arica)* — **the coordinates.** The enneagons: passions, fixations, virtues, holy ideas. What each type is made of. Everything downstream is built on these.

**Claudio Naranjo** *(Character and Neurosis; Ennea-type Structures)* — **the engine.** What the passion is actually doing, and the strategy underneath it. A psychiatrist's read. He learned the enneagrams from Ichazo at Arica around 1970 and developed them along his own line. **Where the base needs to know why a defense runs, this is the source.**

**Riso & Hudson** *(Personality Types; The Wisdom of the Enneagram)* — **the vertical.** The Levels of Development are Riso's own discovery (1977, developed with Hudson in the 1990s) and nobody else has them. **Where the base needs to know how far the grip goes, this is the source.**

**Karen Horney** *(Neurosis and Human Growth, 1950)* — **the social style.** Moving against / away / toward, applied to the Enneagram by Riso & Hudson.

**The honest hierarchy:** Naranjo says *what it is*. Riso says *how bad it's got*. Ichazo says *what it's made of*. They stack.

## What is genuinely ours

Not the facts. **The architecture.**

**1 · The selection.** Riso wrote hundreds of pages per type. Which handful matters *at the moment friction hits* is a judgment the books do not make.

**2 · The re-weighting — the real claim.** Riso puts the contradiction in *"Some Final Thoughts"* — a closing paragraph, a moral, the thing you say at the end. **We put it at the centre and call it load-bearing.** That is a different claim: the contradiction is not the lesson of the type — **it is the mechanism by which the wall is droppable at all.** He wrote it down. We noticed it was the engine.

**3 · The structure.** Eleven fields that make a book operational.

**4 · The vector.** The compression of a doorway into something usable in a live room.

**5 · The application.** Downstream, entirely.

## Known gaps — stated, not filled

**Helen Palmer / the Narrative Tradition.** Palmer's *focus of attention* per type is a distinct cut we do not have. **Parked deliberately, not overlooked:** we hold four lineages covering the coordinates, the engine, the vertical and the social style. Palmer would be a fifth angle on ground already covered from four. If a Palmer cut proves necessary, it gets sourced then. **It is not reconstructed from memory anywhere in the base.**

**Naranjo's DSM correlations.** He mapped types onto DSM personality disorders — **but was not consistent about which disorder went with which type**, the DSM has been revised since, and other teachers read the correlations differently. **The clinical mapping is softer than it looks.** Do not lean on it.

**The instinctual subtypes (27) and the wings.** Never started. Both are real structure in the tradition and both are absent here.

## Build notes — instructions to us, kept out of the base

*These were sitting inside the scientific column, where they read as content. They are not content — they are constraints on how the base gets written. They live here.*

**The tier rule.** Everything in the scientific column is peer-reviewed unless marked otherwise. Two cells are models and say so in plain words. **Established is the default and goes unmarked** — a tag on every cell carries no information and teaches the reader to skip all of them. This is the same ruling as Part 2, applied consistently.

## The copyright rule

Facts are not copyrightable. Expression is. **Everything in the base is the tradition's finding restated in our language.** We cite the finding, never the paragraph.

This is not caution. **It is what makes the base ours rather than a compilation** — and it is why the attribution can live here instead of in the prose. If we were carrying their sentences, we would need their names beside every one. We are not, so we do not.
