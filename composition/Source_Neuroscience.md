## Neural substrate — how threat and regulation actually work

**The amygdala reacts before deliberate appraisal.** **[ESTABLISHED]**
The threat response begins before conscious thought catches up — the amygdala responds to threat cues within roughly 74 ms, while conscious awareness arrives hundreds of milliseconds later. It's a matter of timing, not a hardwired "fear shortcut."
Source: Méndez-Bértolo et al., *Nature Neuroscience* (2016).

**The amygdala detects threat — it is not the "fear center."** **[ESTABLISHED]**
The amygdala detects threat and fires the body's defensive response; the conscious *feeling* of fear is constructed higher up, in cortical circuits. People with amygdala damage still feel fear. Say "threat detector," not "where fear lives."
Source: LeDoux (2015–2019); Feinstein et al., *Nature Neuroscience* (2013).

**The prefrontal cortex can turn the amygdala's alarm down.** **[ESTABLISHED]** *(the load-bearing one)*
The prefrontal cortex down-regulates the amygdala; under threat, that regulation weakens. This is graded down-regulation, **not a toggle** — the two can co-activate, and the PFC exerts both excitatory and inhibitory influence. High anxiety shows a higher-amygdala / lower-PFC pattern (d ≈ 0.99). "Curiosity and defense can't coexist" is false; "engaging regulation dampens the threat response" is true.
Source: prefrontal-tDCS + fMRI in trait anxiety (2017–2019).

## The body — interoception

**Interoception is a real, measurable sense tied to regulation.** **[ESTABLISHED]** *(with one honest edge)*
Interoception (the felt sense of internal bodily state) is real and measurable, tracks with the right anterior insula, and correlates with emotion regulation and stress resilience. The honest edge: evidence that accuracy can be *trained* is early and mixed — a reasonable design bet, not a settled result — and "anxious people have poor interoception" is not clean (heartbeat perception isn't systematically impaired in anxiety). So: assert the sense and its link to regulation; frame trainability as promising, not proven.
Source: Critchley et al. (2004); interoceptive-training review, *Frontiers in Neuroscience* (2022).

## How the mind models the world — predictive coding

**The brain runs on prediction.** **[MODEL]**
A leading model of perception holds that the brain doesn't passively receive the world — it predicts and updates against error. Use it as a lens for why the mind leaves the present to simulate the future. Frame it as "a leading model," not settled fact: its definitions vary and its neural implementation is still debated.
Source: Rao & Ballard (1999); *Annual Review of Neuroscience* (2026).

## Autonomic state — polyvagal

**Vagal tone is real; polyvagal's evolutionary story is not.** **[MODEL]** *(handle with care)*
Cardiac vagal tone (heart-rate variability) is a real, measurable index tied to self-regulation — assert that freely, citing HRV research directly. Polyvagal Theory's *state map* (safety / mobilization / shutdown) can be used as a clinical metaphor, but its core evolutionary and anatomical claims — a myelinated "ventral vagus" as uniquely mammalian and the biological seat of connection — are disputed and, per a 39-author expert consensus, refuted (myelinated vagal fibers exist across many non-mammals). Never present the phylogenetic story as fact.
Source: Grossman et al., *Clinical Neuropsychiatry* (2026, "untenable"); Porges (2026, rebuttal); Taylor et al., *Biological Psychology* (2022). For the real part, cite Thayer & Lane / HRV meta-analyses, **not** polyvagal.

## Body-based trauma work — Somatic Experiencing

**A body therapy with early evidence; its "discharge" mechanism is metaphor.** **[MODEL]**
Somatic Experiencing has preliminary randomized-trial evidence of benefit for PTSD symptoms (one supportive RCT with large effects but a small waitlist sample; a larger, more rigorous trial found no SE-specific advantage). Its mechanism — "trapped survival energy discharged from the body" — is a clinical metaphor with no physiological measurement behind it. You may reference the *practice* and its early evidence; do not assert the *mechanism* as real.
Source: Brom et al., *Journal of Traumatic Stress* (2017); Andersen et al. (2020); Kuhfuß et al. (2021, review).

## Connection between people — synchrony / "limbic resonance"

**Behavioral coordination modestly tracks feeling connected. The rest does not hold.** **[DO NOT STATE]** *(the strong version)*
People who interact often coordinate their behavior, and behavioral synchrony is *modestly* associated with feeling connected and acting prosocially (bonding ≈ .17, prosocial ≈ .28). That much is real and small. Everything beyond it fails: "limbic resonance" is a literary metaphor with no measurement basis; interpersonal *physiological* synchrony has an ambiguous meaning and near-null, direction-inconsistent links to relationship quality; and there is no evidence that two nervous systems "syncing" causes bonding, oxytocin, or dopamine — the oxytocin "bonding hormone" story itself rests on failed replications and unreliable measurement. If connection is discussed, use **coherence** language and keep it to the modest behavioral finding.
Source: Mogan et al. (2017); Gordon & Bartsch, *Nature Reviews Psychology* (2026); Nave et al. (2015, oxytocin).
